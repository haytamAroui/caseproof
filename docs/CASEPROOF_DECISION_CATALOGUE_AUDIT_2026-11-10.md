## Journey and priority gaps

1. **Catalogue completion:** trace actual signed intake, source readback, fidelity projection, human receipt, correction, currentness, release and historical reopening into their concrete APIs and tests. Record allowed states, predicates, reason codes, rule versions, permission checks, and negative evidence for each. This document is a starting audit, not the complete catalogue.
2. **Synthetic end-to-end acceptance:** on an identified commit and environment, execute a signed NL/FR matter through upload, confirmation, check, original-page inspection, reviewer decision, correction, recheck, scoped export and cold reopen. Include wrong source/page, ambiguous mapping, quote mismatch, unreadable scan, missing source, revoked access and changed snapshot. Record actual results and distinguish existing prior receipts from a new run.
3. **Final artifact handoff:** specify and test exact byte matching for a selected file and reviewed export, including an older draft, modified file and DOCX/PDF conversion. A changed file is not automatically a substantive change; no assertion that it was filed.
4. **Coverage language:** inspect every display and export for detected-reference denominators, unscanned regions, omitted sources and unknown recall. Preserve the explicit distinction between review completeness and overall coverage.
5. **Ruleset and replay:** identify decision-affecting versions already in use, then design the proposed canonical manifest and stale-invalidation tests. No unified registry or complete replay is established here.
6. **Claim scope:** decide separately whether to expand beyond the accepted exhibit-reference task. A complete claim inventory, authorized source selection, human decisions and separate claim-review export require contracts and independent assessment; candidate extraction alone is insufficient.
7. **Pilot:** five practitioner interviews and two observed workflows, independent security/privacy and rights review, representative evaluation and firm approval remain open. Synthetic data is not client-data authorization.
